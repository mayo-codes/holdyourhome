🏛️ NYC rent laws at a glance

Rent Stabilized
3% max (1-year lease) or 4.5% max (2-year lease) for leases starting Oct 1, 2025–Sep 30, 2026 (RGB Order #57). Your landlord cannot charge more. If they are, that's a potential overcharge — file with DHCR.
Good Cause (Market Rate)
Since April 2024, many market-rate NYC tenants are now covered by the Good Cause Eviction Law. Rent increases above 5% + CPI (currently ~8–9%) — or 10%, whichever is less — are presumed "unreasonable" and can be challenged in court. Exemptions apply: buildings built after 2009, small landlords (≤10 units), and high-rent units are not covered. Always check your lease for the required Good Cause notice.
No Protections (Market Rate, Exempt)
If your apartment is market-rate and exempt from Good Cause (new building, small landlord, etc.), there is no legal cap on rent increases in NYC. However, you still have the right to negotiate, and your landlord still needs to give proper written notice for any increase over 5% or lease non-renewal (30–90 days depending on tenancy length).

Plain English
Glossary of rental terms

Confused by lease language? Here's what it all actually means.

Rent Stabilization
A NYC system capping annual rent increases and guaranteeing lease renewals for ~1 million apartments. The Rent Guidelines Board (RGB) votes each year on the allowed percentages. For 2025–26: 3% (1-yr) or 4.5% (2-yr).
Rent Control
An older, stricter form of regulation in NYC, separate from stabilization. Applies to a small number of pre-1969 apartments with continuous tenancy. Rent is tied to the "maximum base rent" formula.
RGB (Rent Guidelines Board)
The 9-member NYC body appointed by the Mayor that votes each year on the maximum allowable rent increase for stabilized apartments. Current order: RGB Order #57 (Oct 2025–Sep 2026).
Good Cause Eviction
A 2024 NYS law giving many market-rate NYC renters new protections. Landlords covered by the law need a legal reason to evict and cannot raise rent above ~5% + CPI (or 10%, whichever is lower) without risking a court challenge. Many exemptions apply.
DHCR
Division of Housing and Community Renewal — the NY State agency overseeing rent regulation. You file complaints here for rent overcharges in stabilized apartments. Website: hcr.ny.gov
Preferential Rent
When a stabilized tenant pays below the "legal regulated rent." The landlord may raise to the full legal rent at renewal — a major risk to watch for if you've been paying below market for a stabilized unit.
MCI (Major Capital Improvement)
Building-wide improvements (new roof, boiler, etc.) that allow a landlord to apply for a small permanent rent increase in stabilized buildings, subject to DHCR approval.
IAI (Individual Apartment Improvement)
Work done to your specific unit. Since the 2019 HSTPA, IAI rent increases are capped. Since 2024, the cap was raised to $30,000 (up from $15,000), allowing up to ~$178/mo increase. IAIs are now again permanent and only for vacant apartments.
HSTPA (2019)
Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act — major 2019 law that strengthened tenant rights: eliminated vacancy bonuses, capped IAIs, and closed most paths to deregulation.
Vacancy Bonus
Previously allowed landlords to increase stabilized rents significantly between tenants. Eliminated by the 2019 HSTPA. No longer exists.
Local Rent Standard
Under the Good Cause Eviction law, the "unreasonable" threshold for market-rate apartments: 5% + CPI rate, or 10%, whichever is lower. In 2024, this was ~8.82%.
JustFix
A nonprofit building free tools for NYC tenants. "Who Owns What" (whoownswhat.justfix.org) lets you look up your landlord's full portfolio and whether your building is stabilized.
